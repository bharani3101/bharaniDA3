The charts reveal several clear patterns: family size and number of siblings or spouses aboard rise and fall together, and larger family groups beyond four seem to struggle more, while small groups of two to four enjoyed the best survival odds; paying a higher fare strongly corresponds with traveling in first class and those passengers tended to survive more often, whereas cheaper tickets and third-class travel cluster around non-survivors; women and children appear more frequently among those who lived (especially young children), while adult age groups overlap heavily between survivors and non-survivors; and finally, when you plot age, fare, and family size together, you can see survivors concentrated among higher-fare, smaller-group travelers, whereas low-fare solo passengers are mostly in the non-surviving cluster.
